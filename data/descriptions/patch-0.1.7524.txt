Bow ||| Bow is an engine topic. The bow salvage was done and the bow scale was fixed. With Draw And Release Bows off, holding attack fires again each time the bow is ready, and each click is one draw and one arrow, with the arrow leaving on the release. The Morrowind bow draws properly, the nocked arrow travelling to the string and being drawn back while the bow's limbs and string bend.
Death Penalty ||| The death penalty is the gold lost when dying. It was a quarter of the gold in the purse, and since 0.1.6068 it is a tenth online. Three coins or fewer lose nothing, bank gold and letters of credit are safe, the death screen says exactly how much before the player rises, and a resurrection by a party member costs nothing. Offline, when Project Legacy raises the player after a death they now also lose a tenth of the gold they carry.
Death Screen ||| The death screen was fixed and audited, including resolving the online screen's rise and ensuring quick-loading (F11) after dying in town no longer leaves the death screen up. It shows the amount of gold lost and says Rise instead of "End the journey".
Dungeon Scaling ||| Dungeons answer a strong character. It starts as the player's three best combat or magic skills climb past 75 and needs Master Skills, always on online. At best combat skills of 80 enemies get +2 hit and dodge, health x1.2 and damage x1.05, rising to +36 hit and dodge, x3.4 health and x1.6 damage at 200. How much applies depends on the place: 11% in mines and natural caves, 22% in strongholds, cemeteries and nests, 44% in prisons and harpy and scorpion nests, 88% in crypts and orc and giant strongholds, and 100% in laboratories, covens, vampire haunts and dragon's dens. The wilderness counts too, 22% by day and 44% at night, while weak creatures stay weak and towns never change. Monsters are also tougher by the place they are met, using the grading that decides its loot: in a Crypt the strongest foes get up to a third more health, in a Vampire Haunt up to 70% more, and in a Dragon's Den or the Volcanic Caves up to double health and up to 25% harder blows. Rats, bats and imps gain nothing, and offline, turning off Loot rarity turns the scaling off too.
Elites ||| Elites are tougher special foes. In the open world 5% of foes stand as elites, with no limit of one at a time, after a brief spell at 2% that was undone. They appear only from foe level 3 and never as city watch or allies, ordinary dungeons hold one 20% of the time, and Elite Dungeons keep their 3 to 4. Elites are named everywhere: on hover, in death notices and on corpses. Elites also appear offline while Loot rarity is on, about one foe in twenty in the wilds and sometimes one in a dungeon, and a level-1 or level-2 character meets none among a dungeon's bandits and knights.
Enhanced AI ||| Enhanced AI changes how foes fight. A foe waiting its turn turns and fights when approached instead of backing away, the step back after a blow is under half a second, and hurt foes back off at most once every 10 seconds. An archer waiting its turn stands still facing the player, retreats once for up to 2 seconds when closed on, then fights hand to hand and goes back to shooting once the player is about 6 m away. It is on by default offline as well as online, and can be switched back to the classic enemy AI in Settings > Combat > Enemies, though pressing All off in the features list keeps it off.
Foes ||| Foes under a window were fixed, and guards, bandits, dungeon foes and wilderness encounters now push apart across groups so mixed crowds do not overlap. The wilderness is a little tougher by day and more so at night.
Gem Sockets ||| Weapons carry visible sockets, round wells under the picture on the item card and small pips along the top of the tile in the pack. A weapon's size sets how many it can have: one on a short blade (dagger, tanto, shortsword, wakizashi), two on a one-handed weapon and three on a two-handed weapon, a bow or the Thunderlock, while armour and jewellery keep their single socket. Socketed weapons drop far more often, Magic weapons can have one socket and Rare and Legendary weapons can roll extra sockets up to their size. A gem is set from the pack by clicking an empty socket or choosing Set in... on its card, free apart from the gem being spent and possible on a worn weapon. Clicking a filled socket twice shatters its gem, the Mages Guild's Reforge extracts a gem whole for gold based on its grade, a piece holding a gem is never salvaged, and a set gem adds its worth to the piece's price.
Gems ||| Each of the eight classic gems comes in five grades, Chipped, Flawed, regular, Flawless and Perfect, with better grades giving stronger bonuses. Socketed in a weapon a gem adds fire or frost damage, life leech, extra damage or an attribute, and in armour or jewellery a resistance or an attribute. Gems drop from bodies, dungeon treasure and tavern or guild caches, with deeper and deadlier places dropping better grades, and world bosses drop them too: one from the Oblivion Gate's Warden, one to every player who dealt their share of Sethrakul's damage, and two from the Brass Remnant, all Flawless or Perfect. A weapon's gems of the same kind together give at most one Rare line's worth, so mixing kinds pays more than doubling up, and the item card shows when the limit is hit.
Physical Combat and Armor Overhaul ||| Physical Combat and Armor Overhaul is a mod, version 1.44, ported one to one and re-read method for method. Its archery arm's stock-path hooks and a class enemy's fists were recorded, and the hit-chance clamp was fixed. With it on (the default) an enemy's weapon skill helps it hit exactly as much as the player's helps them and its critical strikes land as often and hit as hard as the player's. A weapon in top condition no longer strikes up to 30% harder and fresh armour no longer lets 15% less damage through unless its Equipment Damage Enhanced module is turned on, though worn gear still hits weaker and blocks less.
