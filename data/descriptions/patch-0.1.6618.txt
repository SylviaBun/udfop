Boarding ||| Boarding is how a ship is captured. After battering a ship until she strikes her colours, the player comes alongside, slows right down (heaving to beside her now stops the player within boarding reach, even at storm speeds) and presses Activate to throw grapples, which puts out her fires. Her crew and captain fight on her deck, and killing the captain and most of the crew takes her. The player's own crew comes along, and the player lands across from where they stood. Pirates can board the player too, with only one pirate raid at a time, and they fall back and cast off if beaten or timed out, then wait five minutes before grappling again.
Bring Online ||| Bringing a character online starts it in the world's present and it is never charged for time away. An offline character with Oblivion leveling switches to Daggerfall leveling when brought online, starting from its current level, with level, skills and any earned level-up carried over and the skill bar starting empty. A Never saved character's tile says Undo bringing in, which gives back everything that came with it, and a message says when a character was played on another of the player's accounts.
Character Creation ||| Character creation is the screen where a new character is made. The creator tells the player what they rolled, a phone version exists, and a character made online no longer gets a skill check the moment it is created. The Project Legacy question (Enduring, Bloodline, No lineage) and the leveling question are Enhanced Plus popups in which the option under the pointer is the one highlighted and clicking it selects it.
Chat Window ||| The chat window had a run of fixes. The closing Enter belongs to the window, the chat list holds its rows, the hide button survives a frame, a doubled line was removed and the chat fits its space. The player list no longer rebuilds and re-sorts every frame while the chat is open, and the friends list sorts faster.
Cite Tool ||| The cite tool is development tooling. AUDIT 64's cite mapper and its Ledger arm landed as one tool and main was merged with cites by provenance. Since 0.1.6570 the developers' notes and code comments that point into the game's code name the code itself instead of a line number, so updates no longer collide over them, and hundreds of pointers that had drifted onto the wrong code were corrected.
Courtship and Marriage ||| Courtship and marriage belong to Project Legacy. A townsperson who counts the player a friend can be courted by asking about Courtship in conversation, once a day, depending on Personality, Etiquette and tone, and when their heart is full the player can ask for their hand and is wed at the temple of their town. The spouse joins the house, keeping their own face and day, and every thirty days there is a one-in-four chance of a child, at most six with each spouse, who cannot be played until the mantle passes to them. If someone being courted dies the courtship ends. Online, two players can wed by standing in a temple together and pressing Propose marriage on the other, each keeping their own house, and word of a spouse's death or deletion reaches the player the next time they are online.
Crew ||| The crew are the named hands on the player's ship. Each near crew member is a real animated character with a green health bar, a name and a job such as First Mate, Bard, Bosun, Gunner or Carpenter, remembers fights and boardings, and chats in pairs. The crew strike up a sea shanty led by a bard, hurry about when the guns are out, and line the rail when grapples are thrown. The crew lasts twice as long as before, since cannon fire, fires on deck, rams and the sea serpent's blows take half as many men, though swivel-gun boats still go for the crew first at half the old pace and enemy crews are unchanged. Hands lost in a boarding cost the ship that much crew, the player's own blows and spells pass their crew by, and the player cannot pickpocket their own crew or a town's defenders.
Crowds ||| Crowds of players are drawn much more smoothly with the Morrowind bodies, and a nearer player taking over a body slot waits a moment instead of swapping constantly. Distant players in a big crowd keep walking smoothly even when their computer runs at a low frame rate.
Decorate ||| Decorate is the house furnishing mode. Doors are in its catalogue, and Daggerfall's interior doors can be hung in any open doorway, with every free doorway marked green and the one looked at gold. A door hangs only where it can close, stays open, shut or locked as left, can be moved, and blocks the way while shut, and it parts the rooms either side, making two rooms for the room tabs and renting. In the In this room and In this yard views, Turn left and Turn right turn a placed piece 15 degrees where it stands, at once and for free, and a flat picture flips to face the other way.
Family Tab ||| The Family tab is part of Project Legacy, in the pause window between Holdings and System. It has the Tree (zoom, pan, play as a sibling, pass the mantle, and where each member lives), the House (model, seat, generations, the living and the fallen, and the family's homes) and the Hall of Ancestors (every house founded), and keypad / opens it. The character sheet on the Stats tab also shows the house, its model and the player's generation and, in an Enduring house, the age against the lifespan, the years Arkay has taken and when the player becomes an elder.
Family in the World ||| Under Project Legacy, family members who are not being played live in the world: in the family home chosen on the Family tab's House page or, with no house, in a house the family's seat town lends them. They keep a daily routine like the town's other residents, asleep at home at night and out in the market and streets by day. One already played appears in their own body with their race and last-saved gear, while one never played wears the town's own clothes. Talking to one greets the player as family and offers Play as, a normal Talk or Goodbye, saving the current character first. A character saved in one of the family's houses waits there, and one saved elsewhere is on their own journey until played again. A family member the player kills, or a monster kills in the street, is dead for good. It is on by default and can be turned off with Family In World in Project Legacy's settings.
Heirlooms ||| Heirlooms belong to Project Legacy. A fallen character may leave an heirloom, their most valuable worn weapon or armour, renamed for the house, and their remains lie where they fell with the heirloom and part of their purse. The heir gets the death quest The Bones of (name), shown in the journal and circled on the map, and the remains open like a container on arrival. Sold or dropped bones return to where the ancestor fell. Laying the remains to rest at a temple or the family seat gives the ancestor's blessing (+3 to their best skill, at most +9 on any one skill) and makes the heirloom stronger with each generation that carries it home. An heirloom cannot be taken twice by rewinding or switching family members.
Helm Controls ||| At the helm the arrow keys steer: up makes more sail, down takes it in, and left and right steer, and the arrows no longer turn the view. W/S and up/down do the same, one step along a single ladder. Under Responsive handling the helm trims the sails itself, so the trim buttons and keys are gone. When in irons the helm tells the player how to get out, and gamepad steering is no longer taken over by the boat menu when looking down.
Helm Panel ||| At the helm a panel shows the ship's hull, sails, crew, guns reloading, waters and notoriety, and the ship's speed through the water in knots beside her name. It shows the controls at a glance with a sails button that uses the arrows, and during an Overworld sea crossing the panel and sail keys stay out of the journey's way. On a phone or tablet it has its own Brace button, holds in two rows of helm buttons and sits above the touch buttons with tap hints.
Leveling ||| Leveling is chosen at character creation on the "How will you grow?" screen, in the Enhanced Plus look. Online characters level the Daggerfall way, with Oblivion Remastered leveling shown locked as "Offline characters only", and online characters that were on Oblivion leveling switch to Daggerfall leveling the next time they are played, keeping their level, skills and any earned level-up. Offline the player can still pick either system.
Login ||| A crash some online players hit right after logging in was fixed, and logging in no longer sends the player back to the title screen after the account service fell over under load. In 0.1.6566 logins stopped failing for everyone after the account server was overwhelmed by every game checking every town's yard decorations each minute: yards are now fetched only for towns near the player, the server keeps a short copy, and a failed fetch waits longer each time.
Online Players ||| Other players are drawn as paperdolls with names or in the Morrowind body, one rig instance per peer built from their look, third-person at the feet. They no longer jump backwards near an area edge, teleport in sieges, Royal Tourneys, homes and ships, dash ahead after a lag spike or snap back when stepping into a new area, because movement updates carry their send time. When a player's movement stops reaching you they now stand still until they move again instead of running in place, including players who went quiet before you arrived.
Online Sea ||| Everyone nearby shares one online sea with the same ships, broadsides and sinkings, which another player's game takes over if its host dies or goes indoors. Pirates attack every player's boat but players cannot hurt each other's boats, and a navy ship that sees any player fire on a merchantman turns on them, judging each by their own notoriety. Boarding is shared and everyone in the room sees the fight on deck with the crew as allies with green bars. When players share a sea it has the fewest ships any of them chose under Ships at sea, a sunk ship's floating cargo shows for every player with each cask going to whoever reaches it first, and other players stand on a ship's deck as it sails and rocks. In a storm a galleon or Carrack no longer vanishes from other players' screens, and a short connection hiccup no longer puts a player off a friend's ship they are riding.
Performance ||| A GPU memory leak from opening the map in a dungeon or building was fixed, long sessions no longer pile up GPU memory since places left are released after a while, and people, guards and foes no longer pop out of view at the edges of the screen. Settings are also read a little faster everywhere.
Project Legacy ||| Project Legacy, by Lattymoy, Ghð§† and Positronico, is part of the game and on by default (off on its tile in Features). Every new character founds a house with a surname and a seat in the first town they stand in, often with a brother or sister, unless the player chooses No lineage online. The player chooses how death works: Enduring costs years of life taken by Arkay and lets the line carry on to an heir, Bloodline makes death final with a Succession window to pick who takes the mantle, and No lineage (online) means no house and death as it always was. Heirs are born with stats, skills, career and race drawn from their parents' and a share of the family estate of up to 9,900 gold, and a child inherits part of a parent's reputation. Towns talk about the house, and the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online the family lives in the realm and the house's name shows under the player's name, with a skull marking a Bloodline; characters made before the update have no house online. Permadeath cannot be undone by quick-loading, closing the game or a second tab, and a fallen character's Stores, professions and market place fall with them. See also Heirlooms, Courtship and Marriage, Family in the World and Family Tab.
Quests ||| Quests received many fixes. A quest monster killed by a party member counts for the player's quest, a quest's monsters are the player's own and players outside the party cannot see or hit them, and a quest's hours, date and season follow the sky with a daily window recurring every 30 real minutes. Quest-givers are back, every party member's copy of a shared quest picks the same random monsters, and quest items in the world show their name when looked at. Quests that would not start in towns redesigned by Beautiful Villages and Beautiful Cities now start by using the nearest town that has a missing building, quest characters whose building is gone live in a house of their own town, and shared party quests no longer move to another building on every sync. Individual quests fixed include the Bodyguard quest, a knightly order's artifact hunt, the Totem of Tiber Septim, The Qualifying Examination, The Possessed Child and Pothago's Dark Brotherhood contract.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir.
Sailing ||| Players can sail together and set sail from their own ship, and sailing from the Overworld tells the player how many days of their time the voyage takes. Overworld travel by sea sails straight for a port that lies upwind instead of zig-zagging, steers well clear of islands at the new speeds and sails through fog instead of rowing.
Sails ||| Sails no longer flap uselessly or blow backwards when the ship points into the wind, and a gaff, jib or lateen sail bellies away from the wind on either tack. The ship can no longer get stuck in irons.
Sea Serpent ||| Every four hours Sethrakul the Old Coil, a great serpent, rises on a packet lane in open sea. Harbour bells ring fifteen minutes before, chat names the port and the map rings its waters, and players sail out and fight it with their ships' guns, each ship that joins making it tougher. It lashes with its tail, rams down a lane, bursts up under its mark and spits venom, from two-thirds health the Coil wraps a ship (broken by focus fire to stun it), and from one-third the Maelstrom drags ships toward a whirlpool's eye. It no longer teleports but swims every move, with a wake showing where it is going, and aims where the player's ship will be when an attack lands, surging at the player on the surface when out of reach and giving up after about 12 seconds of being outsailed. While exactly two ships fight it its attacks deal two thirds of their damage, so two ships can win, though one alone cannot in fair weather and three or more take full damage. A broadside is judged where the serpent was when the shot landed. A storm closes its waters fifteen minutes after it rises and ten minutes before it dives, every ship whose guns did a tenth of her share earns a hoard of gold and Magic-or-better pieces including a Deadlands Ember, and each serpent slain is worth six quests of Renown and pays 40 silver (20 for a ship standing by), toward the same 150 silver a day.
Ship Handling ||| Ships sail on every heading. Pointed straight into the wind a ship makes a little over half her best speed, where before she stalled or was blown backwards, and she is fastest with the wind behind her off to one side, with no more tacking. Ships are faster under sail (a galleon with the wind on her side nearly two thirds faster), a storm drives her harder up to twice a fair breeze, and in fog she keeps nearly a third of her speed. They pick up speed in about 7 seconds instead of 25, slow to boarding speed in about 8, and turn on a wider circle at the faster speed. The rudder works at a standstill and bites hardest at half sail, rowing after striking sail no longer holds speed, and AI ships turn and gather speed at the player's own ship's pace. Classic handling is one switch away in Features, Naval Combat, Ship handling. Ships no longer stick on rocks or in place at sea, and one run aground can float off.
Small Ship ||| The Small Ship is a brand-new galleon model by Mac, the hull the pirate brigantines, merchant galleons and navy cutters sail. Hatch covers lift open with ladders to the gun deck and cabin and bulkhead doors swing, her wheel stands on the castle roof where the player steers and turns with the rudder, and she has five sails that furl, set, fill with wind and swing as trimmed. She is painted with gilt bands, a panelled castle, glowing stern gallery and red gunport shutters, and has five gunports a side whose shutters swing up and guns run out for a broadside. With one gun fewer than the old galleon, the player's galleon's balls now do 20% more damage to hulls and sails so her broadside hits like six guns, and pirates size her up by it, while enemy captains' galleons are unchanged. At a quay her gangway climbs to the entry port in her waist, and if her model fails to load the old galleon takes her place.
Steady Shadows ||| Steady Shadows is a switch in the Enhanced lighting tile, on by default, that stops shadows popping on and off by redrawing every shadow every frame. The nearest lamps keeping full shadows went from 8 to 12, lamps at almost the same distance no longer trade shadows, the player's own shadow no longer jumps from lamp to lamp, and eyes adjust to light changes more slowly. It costs graphics work and about 48 MB of memory, so it can be turned off if frame rate drops, and the player's silhouette falls only from the two nearest lamps. Lantern shadows now cost much less to prepare, since each lantern looks once at what can reach it and a still scene does not look at all.
Temples ||| Libraries now open at the rank the temple promises, such as Curate, rank 4, for Stendarr. A donation priest restores five points of standing with the region's law for a rising price, and paying the temple ends the conversation so one more key press no longer gives away another 1,000 gold. Every Beautiful Villages temple has its god's Daedra summoner again, standing apart from the priest so clicking the priest always reaches the priest.
Yard ||| A yard is the ground around the player's house. Standing on their own lot and pressing Decorate places catalogue pieces outside, priced as inside, wholly on the lot, up to 60 pieces a yard, seen by everyone walking the street. A piece can stand at most 4 m above the ground, so stacking towers is no longer possible, though pieces already higher stay put and can be moved down or removed. Outside, pieces hold nothing and give no light and doors are for doorways only, a piece on the road is refused, and a long piece turned at an angle can no longer be placed through the house's corner.
