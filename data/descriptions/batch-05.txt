Naval Combat ||| Naval combat lets boats with guns fight, and needs Come Sail Away. The Large Boat carries swivel guns, the Small Ship and the Carrack carry long guns with bow chain-shot chasers and stern fire barrels, and the Large Galley carries long guns along her sides and great guns over her bow. At the helm the player looks to a side and holds Attack, then lets go to fire the broadside, looking higher to fire further, and holds Crouch to brace behind the rail for less damage but guns that hold fire. Each side reloads on its own, with a thin crew slower and a boat with no crew slowest, and the player's gunners lose accuracy as crew are killed. Cannon balls hole the hull, cut sails and kill crew, with hits below the waterline doing most damage, and a ship can catch fire. Features, Naval Combat has settings for ship count, pirates boarding, raiders' plunder and the broadside camera, and a series of changes made busy bays run much smoother.
Naval Law ||| Firing on a merchantman or a navy ship is piracy, and notoriety in its waters rises and fades daily, shown as anchors on the ship's panel. Sinking or taking a pirate earns standing with that kingdom and the knightly orders, and a pirate flagship also earns standing with the temples.

Nearby Quests ||| Nearby Quests is a switch, on by default, that sends quests to towns and dungeons near where they were taken. At level 1 a quest stays within about 10 map squares, a day or two on foot, each level adds 4 squares, and by level 15 or so anywhere in the region can come up. If fewer than three dungeons of the right kind are close it picks from the nearest three, a town quest needing a missing building looks twice as far then anywhere in the region, and quests already held keep their places. Off is Daggerfall's pick, and online each player's own switch decides.

Notice Board ||| The Notice Board is the corkboard that a town's rumour boards open, in Enhanced Plus style. It shows town news, a pointer to the Bounty Board, server notices under a red seal and then players' notes, and every rumour board in a town shows the same notes. "N new" floats over a board with unread notes as the player approaches. A registered player can pin a note of up to 800 characters for 1, 3 or 7 days, up to 3 across every board, which can carry a button to ask to join the party or the guild, or a Challenge to a duel. Only a Guildmaster or an Officer who can invite can pin a guild recruitment note. A note can be taken down at any time by its author, any registered player can report one, and after three different reports it is hidden until a moderator looks, with moderators able to remove any note and a muted player unable to pin. Many fixes keep half-written forms and notes from being lost.

Oblivion Gates ||| Oblivion Gates are nightly raid events built around the marked Warden. His fight crosses three courts, ending in a wipe that must be broken, with two new courts as large as the first. In the last court he calls Dagon's Reckoning every minute or so, which lands on the whole arena unless the 3 to 8 Crystals of Oblivion that grow while he calls it are all broken, stunning him for 8 seconds with every hit then doing 50% more. His aspect and trials show under his health bar, a card shows tonight's marks before the fight, burning ground hits harder and he hits about a third harder, with telegraphs clearer. Loot spreads out when he falls, can be picked up with activate, and his chest bursts in gold, and when he falls a damage chart ranks everyone in the fight for a minute. The boss music is louder and darker, in D minor and B-flat minor with low brass and heavy drums. Closing a gate records it and pays its Marks.
