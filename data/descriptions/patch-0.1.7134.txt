Abyss Dungeons ||| An Abyss Dungeon is the hardest dungeon in the game, online only. One at a time rises beside a busy city in the region with the most players online, with a column of brass-gold light over it, and the first player to reach its door finds it and everyone online hears where, after which its ring, compass mark, Timers row and notice-board note stay until it is gone. It has three foes at every spot, each four times as tough and hitting two and a half times as hard, six elite foes, half again the loot and no campfires. At its deepest point stands the Rift into the Shattered Hour, where the Orrery of Endings, the Unmoored Steps and the Brass Remnant are met, and the Remnant's fight, spoils, collapse and one-life rule are shared by everyone in the Hour at once. Each Abyss Dungeon keeps one of six Endings and two of nine omens, and fighters earn Hours broken toward the Hourbreaker title and The Turning Hour aura. Crew and sworn companions wait outside the Rift, and leaving the Hour for any reason puts the player on the ground at the dungeon's door.
Accounts ||| Accounts hold a player's login and their cloud saves, served by an account Worker that issues tokens and checks passwords. The service was built in 0.1.3607 and 0.1.3690, then repaired after an outage caused by an overly costly password-hashing setting on Cloudflare, and later given a clock fix. The account server has since been protected from overload: after it fell over again in 0.1.6625 games now wait longer after each failed ask instead of retrying at once, and in 0.1.6640 signing in, joining a room and loading a town's house layouts were made far cheaper for it. The letterbox, the count over a town's Notice Boards and the play time now share one request to the account service instead of three, cutting its database work by about a third per player.
Auctions ||| Auctions let a player sell a Masterwork piece to the highest bidder. The opening bid is 1 to 1,000,000 Marks with a fee of 1% of it, and an auction stands 24 hours in its region and counts among the seller's 30 listings. Each bid must beat the standing one by 5% and at least 1 Mark, and a bid in the last 2 minutes adds 2 minutes. The winning bid pays the seller less a 5% sales tax, and the piece goes to the winner at once at a board in the region, otherwise after the courier's time. Bids are held from the bidder's Marks and returned if outbid, and a piece with no bids comes back. An auction won in a town whose seat takes a Tithe now closes on time and pays its seller, and auctions, guild writs and contracts settle on the service's own clock even when nobody opens the market or the Work tab.
Brass of Numidium ||| The Brass of Numidium is a new Aetheric set of nine Dwarven brass pieces, every one at the top of the Legendary range, with set bonuses Dwemer Brass, Gearward and The Hour Turns. Only the Brass Remnant's spoils carry it, one time in three.
Crouch ||| C crouches and sneaks: while crouched the player moves at sneaking pace and stealth checks apply, so enemies are slower to notice them. Left Alt no longer sneaks, the old standing sneak stays in Controls as an optional key with no key set by default, and players with saved key bindings see a one-time note that Sneak lost its key. A crouch that would not undo was fixed earlier.
Cursor ||| The cursor is no longer shown off the HUD, and hovering a menu scrollbar or a screen edge no longer swaps the game cursor for the system arrow. On a controller the menu cursor eases in, speeds up on a long full push, slows over buttons and settles onto the nearest one on release, in every menu on both skins. At the main menu the left stick moves the same cursor and A presses what it is on, and Settings > Controls > Controller cursor assist > Off gives back Daggerfall's plain fixed-speed cursor. Clicking with it no longer acts like a held-down drag in some windows.
Dungeon Map ||| The dungeon map was reworked as an enhanced map with a 3D view, where holding the right mouse button turns and tilts the map at the same time and steep ramps in hallways fill in once walked. Selecting Info mode underground lights up for six seconds what is near, in sight and nameable within 8 m, and a secrets tier shows hidden movers that only a lever chain reaches. A lever that moves something out of sight marks the spot on the map until found, and its plaque in Info mode says which way its work lies. The compass points back to the entrance along the trail walked, optional exact quest guidance marks a quest's people and things on the map and compass, and secret walls are no longer labelled "Door".
Dungeon Respawn ||| A dungeon's dead stay dead. Ordinary dungeons outside the Wrothgarian zone respawn after 20 minutes and elite dungeons after 40.
Dungeon Sizes ||| Online, every dungeon has its own size, small, medium or large, the same for everyone in it: small ones stay small, about half of all are medium, and the main story's dungeons and Abyss Dungeons are always large. Offline, a Medium Dungeons option builds dungeons at a medium size of two interior blocks closed by six, and another option uses the online sizes offline, with a quest keeping the size it was set up at.
Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons.
Dwarven Thunderlock ||| The Dwarven Thunderlock is a gun, a new weapon type prototyped in a lab and integrated into the game. It works in the Morrowind arm rig, with its icons, paperdoll grip, cadence, flash and shot all fixed to match the prototype. Dwemer Pellets are sold wherever weapons are, in Weapon Smiths, Armorers, General Stores and Pawn Shops, and restocked each day. The gun and its pellets show their own art in the pack, on the hotbar and in the quick slots, and a list shows the whole gun. The Morrowind 3D gun has no holes left, kicks back at the shot, and tips down and back up on reload with the breech heard after the bang.
Enhanced Maps ||| Enhanced maps put one map on three sheets with a tab strip, derived floors, a 2D automap and a coloured town. Zooming the enhanced map out past the Iliac Bay shows the whole of Tamriel in the same hand, with nine provinces and borders, capitals, mountains, rivers and seas, and hovering reads the province, a capital or the sea; nothing beyond the Bay can be travelled to. The continent is traced from the game's own map of Tamriel (the race screen's picture) as one continent with the Imperial Province in the middle, with the Bay placed on it by matching its own coastline.
Features ||| Features is the screen where every enhanceable feature is switched. It is a panel of tiles with on and off shown as green and red, an All off button and a search. Descriptions are in plain words, and Daggerfall Unity's own options end with their default, such as "Off by default in Daggerfall Unity." Under Combat, a Steel Helm switch picks Closed (a visor and a blue plume, the default) or Open (a nasal helm with a mail aventail that shows the face). Snowfall, Windfall and Heat Haze each have a switch, on by default in the enhanced outdoors, while the desert sand and winter ground are packs of their own on the Replacement packs card. The Tamriel on the map and Land beyond the Bay rows are on by default on the enhanced skin.
Heat Haze ||| In the deserts by day the distant land and sky shimmer in the heat, fully under a clear sky and half as much under cloud, fading in after sunrise and out before sunset. It has its own switch in Features, on by default in the enhanced outdoors.
Hourlock ||| The Hourlock is a Gilded Thunderlock, what the Warp kept of a Dwemer smith's last commission, with fixed lines of +40% damage, +15 Agility, +30 Archery and +10 shock damage a shot, each at the top of the Legendary range. Every third shot that lands strikes for double and a foe it fells gives back the pellet that felled it. It lands about twice what any Legendary Thunderlock does a shot and is still the slowest weapon to fire, and it cannot be broken down, reforged, honed, cursed, socketed or made an heirloom. Its power sleeps in a duel, and its first find joins the codex at the head of the list. It comes from the Brass Remnant's spoils one time in fifty, online.
Iliac Hand ||| The Iliac Hand is a two-player collectible card game played on a tavern's card table: three holdings between the players, six turns, hold two of three to win. It has units, spells and Daedric Prince cards, painted in code with the Bay's creatures, artifacts and places. Every character carries a Card Binder from creation that holds their decks, and a Collections tab under Holdings shows the weapon codex and cards side by side.
Land Beyond the Bay ||| Walking off the edge of the Iliac Bay, the land of Tamriel continues as hills, mountains and coasts under each province's own sky and trees, as far as the player cares to walk. It is empty ground for now, with no towns, roads or dungeons, and it follows the same traced land as the map. It is a Features row, on by default on the enhanced skin. A freeze on going outside, caused by the online relay's code being pulled into the terrain worker, was fixed.
Launcher ||| The desktop app opens on a launcher with patch notes, options and Play, and nothing starts until Play. Updates download, install and reopen before play, so the player plays on the same version as everyone online, or Play without updating installs it on quit. New versions are marked New and one on its way Update. Options cover the game files folder, saves folder, update check and Reinstall, and on first run it looks for Daggerfall itself. A chosen folder is used only when whole, and a folder missing files says which, and on a Mac it points to DaggerfallGameFiles.zip. The launcher can be used with a controller: D-pad or left stick to move, A or Start to press, B to go back. Adding --play to the launch options (Playnite, Steam shortcuts, Big Picture) skips the launcher and goes straight into the game, with updates still arriving in-game and installing on quit; the launcher still opens on the first run or if the Daggerfall folder cannot be found.
LeFay Monument ||| A monument to Julian LeFay, one of the creators of Daggerfall, stands in the middle of Gothway Garden, with three granite steps, a marble pedestal, a gilt-tipped obelisk and a bronze plaque on each side. Looking at it offers Throw flowers or Read the inscription, and in Info mode a press reads the inscription. A thrown flower lands on the steps or at the foot on the player's side, each character keeps its 48 newest flowers through save and load, and the plaque counts how many were laid. Townspeople walk around it, and online the flowers are the player's alone while others see the monument.
Loot Rarity ||| Loot rarity is a Diablo-style ladder over Daggerfall's loot, built on Unleveled Loot's law and on by default, with the ladder shown on the items. Clothing can drop as Magic and Rare with lines of its own: standing with a social group, warmth and weatherproofing (for Climates & Calories), Personality, and street skills, with footwear leaning to movement skills. Superior and Masterwork clothing the player makes comes out Magic and Rare, as crafted weapons and armour already did. Six Legendary garments each have a power: the Alik'r Wayfarer's Robes (heat felt 20 less), the Wrothgar Pass Mantle (cold felt 20 less), the Vestments of Stendarr (Restoration spells cost 25% less), the Pilgrim's Sandals (tire 25% slower, falls hurt half as much), the Silks of Queen Barenziah (+5 standing with every group) and the Shade's Cowl (+25 Stealth while hooded). With the loot rarity setting off, clothing stays exactly as in Daggerfall. Resistance from rolled gear counts up to 45% per element, and only fills an Aetheric piece's resistance up to that same 45%. Hone raises one line within its own range, and some Rares and Legendaries carry an empty socket. One Rare or Legendary item in twelve from a body or a pile is cursed, with one extra bonus line and one drawback: a bad reputation, bad reactions, damage or wear in holy places, weaker blows against a kind of foe, or a health leech. Gilded is the new top of the rarity ladder, above Aetheric and Artifacts, with its name in gold leaf and a frame breathing light in every slot. A rarity affix of +N% casting speed rolls on jewellery and weapons and adds up across everything worn, up to 30%.
Mode Wheel ||| Holding Left Alt opens a wheel of interaction modes: Talk (up), Grab (right), Steal (down) and Info (left). Flicking the mouse toward a mode and letting go or clicking chooses it, and moving back to the middle cancels. F1 through F4 no longer change modes by default or open the browser's help or find bar, and can be bound back to the four modes in Controls. On a controller the d-pad's next mode still cycles through the modes.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains.
Project Legacy ||| Project Legacy, by Lattymoy, Ghð§† and Positronico, is part of the game and on by default (off on its tile in Features). Every new character founds a house with a surname and a seat in the first town they stand in, often with a brother or sister, unless the player chooses No lineage online. The player chooses how death works: Enduring costs years of life taken by Arkay and lets the line carry on to an heir, Bloodline makes death final with a Succession window to pick who takes the mantle, and No lineage (online) means no house and death as it always was. Heirs are born with stats, skills, career and race drawn from their parents' and a share of the family estate of up to 9,900 gold, and a child inherits part of a parent's reputation. Towns talk about the house, and the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online the family lives in the realm and the house's name shows under the player's name, with a skull marking a Bloodline; characters made before the update have no house online, and a family that has not changed since the last checkpoint is no longer uploaded again. Permadeath cannot be undone by quick-loading, closing the game or a second tab, and a fallen character's Stores, professions and market place fall with them. An heir's bequest gets the same load fixes as other items when it is paid, and a fallen relative's remains get them when opened. See also Heirlooms, Courtship and Marriage, Family in the World and Family Tab. In an Enduring family a vampire, werewolf or wereboar who dies loses no years to Arkay's toll and always rises even with their span spent, and once cured the toll applies again from the next death.
Resting ||| Online, the player rests at a lit fire, a bed (room, house, ship's cabin or dungeon bed on their floor) or a guild hall that allows it. They hold still six seconds, the rest breaks when an enemy comes, they are hurt or the fire goes out, and when the bar fills they sleep a whole night, eight hours on their own clock, in one step, healing health, stamina and magicka as it fills. A night comes once every ten minutes of play, resting sooner being a short rest that heals and passes no time, and the enhanced HUD's status bar shows a Rested tile counting minutes to the next night. A night at a fire, tent or bed heals fully while a guild hall floor is rough, monsters can still find the player at night, and the loiter option is gone online since the sky runs on real time. Resting ages spells, diseases, hunger and skill checks as an hour of rest should. Resting while poisoned or diseased warns the player first.
Shop Screen ||| The shop screen's inherited half was fixed. Hovering an item in any shop shows the same card as in the inventory.
Snowfall ||| In winter, outside the deserts, real snow lies on the ground, deeper in the wild than in towns, building up while snow falls and melting back in clear weather. The player, monsters, guards and townsfolk leave tracks in it, which slowly fill back in (faster while it snows) and are kept in the save, and a fallen enemy leaves a hollow where it lay. With Basic Roads, paths and tracks keep only a thin layer of snow and main roads get a bank along their edges, and the winter ground under it has new textures. It has its own switch in Features, on by default in the enhanced outdoors.
Spellbook ||| The spellbook gesture was recorded, and the Icon button lets the player pick any icon for a spell, or none so it shows its initials. The spellbook can no longer be dropped, put in a chest, pile or companion's pack, shift-dropped or sold at a shop, though it still goes into the player's own wagon, with the message "Your spellbook never leaves you."
Spells ||| Ending the player's own spells was fixed, the aim hint always shows when a spell waits for a target, and a party member healing the player no longer repeats the cast message on every cast. Classic spellcasting runs at the same pace as the Morrowind animation, with the spell leaving the hands at half a second, and a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, so clicking or recasting during it does nothing and costs nothing. Casting speed scales with the Speed attribute, up to 25% faster at high Speed and 25% slower at low. Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects.
Stats Card ||| The enhanced paperdoll card has a Stats button that flips it to a stats page. The back shows damage, hit chance, critical chance and what a critical does, backstab chance and expected damage, with a table per swing direction (sideways, thrust, down-right, down-left, overhead) and a bow's release, and a breakdown of weapon roll, material, Strength, proficiency, race, attack term, foe armour and dodging. Defence shows overall armour, the seven part numbers, dodging, biography and health with attributes and skills, and hit and damage are scored against a foe of the player's level in four armour classes using real armour numbers. It is Enhanced skin only. The stats screen shows resistances.
Tavern Cards ||| Every tavern and inn has its own card table, a green-felt poker table with six stools standing near the entrance, and pressing activate there sits the player down to play Texas Hold'em with the tavern's regulars or with other players online. The character is seated for everyone to see, and the cards are physical on the cloth: dealt, fanned, peeked at, chips dragged to bet and the deck riffled. Online the relay deals, owning the shuffle, hole cards and seat clock so no client can see another's hand, and gold tables take a buy-in of real gold held in escrow by the account service and paid out on the hand's receipt. The tavern's ordinary dining tables are no longer card tables.
Temples ||| Libraries now open at the rank the temple promises, such as Curate, rank 4, for Stendarr. A donation priest restores five points of standing with the region's law for a rising price, and paying the temple ends the conversation so one more key press no longer gives away another 1,000 gold. Every Beautiful Villages temple has its god's Daedra summoner again, standing apart from the priest so clicking the priest always reaches the priest. Temples offer Heal Curse beside Heal Disease.
Textures ||| Textures had incident fixes, one shape for every texture door, and performance work that deleted work instead of reducing quality (PERF-TEX/TEX2/UI and MENU1-WARM). The steel plate cuirass has its skirt, painted with its own texture. The desert ground has new sand textures with ripples, from Sands of the Alik'r.
Town Watch ||| The town watch keeps the town day and night in three eight-hour shifts plus a day off. Patrols walk in pairs around their own district, in larger towns a watchman stands at each gate, off duty he wears his own clothes as a townsperson, and larger towns keep a larger watch. In the 14 cities whose palace stands in its own walled grounds the watch now lives where its door opens onto the streets, so it walks its patrols instead of staying at the palace. A watchman on duty greets the player by their standing with the region's law, a first minor offence in a region draws a Hold! warning and not an arrest (the next brings one), and the watch shouts "Halt!" from one watchman at most once every 15 seconds. A monster that gets into town can attack townspeople, one blow killing one, and the watch defends them as it does the player. Paying a fine no longer counts as killing the watchman who walked away. All of this is in the living world, and Classic play keeps Daggerfall's own watch and townspeople. The watch posted at a city's gates stops strangers coming in and asks their business, and strangers answer civilly, curtly or with open hostility, sellswords and sailors rougher; patrols stop strangers on their rounds, every stranger stopped at a gate is questioned, and a gate stop no longer keeps a visitor from leaving. The watch breaks up a quarrel even at a change of shift, and only one of a patrol pair calls the hour.
Townspeople ||| Townspeople in the living world run from a monster that comes close, along the street rather than through walls, and keep their distance. Those who stop to talk turn to face each other, a group waits until all have walked over and then takes turns, and the player hears them only then. People keep the same company about two minutes before moving on, with much less repetition, and indoors people at a table start talking as soon as they sit together. A stranger who said nothing as the player passed now speaks when the player stops in front of them, and a townsperson who stops for the player stays exactly where they stopped. They no longer stand inside walls or out over the harbour water or walk on the spot, and since 0.1.6640 they keep room from one another: people waiting at one spot keep a little distance, two leaving a shop together walk out in single file, and a walker steps aside, keeping right. Each square has several gathering points and every neighbourhood has a corner where its people meet, stall-keepers set up at the square or their own market, and people head out for the evening and the morning market at their own time. Guild members, priests, adventurers, sellswords and couriers walk the streets in their class, and someone keeping their place on their own appears as one of Daggerfall's own pictures of their kind, becoming themselves again the moment they walk on or join a conversation. A cat that meowed many times a second while the player looked at certain townspeople was fixed. Townsfolk inside a building spread through the whole room and sit at tables set apart, never at a table split by a wall, nobody waits at a tavern for hours for lunch, and everyone in a room sees the same people in the same places. In the street townsfolk fall out with each other, more often in the evening between drinkers, buyers haggle at stalls, beggars ask for a coin, and when someone shouts the people nearby fall quiet and turn to look. Small voices fill the street (the night watch calling the hour, stalls crying their wares, beggars calling out, drinkers singing home), every player in a town sees the same incidents, and beggars and stall keepers turn to face whoever they are talking to.
Wallet ||| Every character carries a Wallet, which new characters start with and existing ones received on next load. It holds letters of credit, Deadlands Embers and Welkynd Shards and shows gold and, online, silver. Its sheet is a ledger: Coin (gold, silver) and In the wallet (letters of credit, Deadlands Embers, Welkynd Shards), with each amount lined up on the right. In the Enhanced inventory the three move to the Wallet on Valuables, in the classic inventory nothing moves and the Wallet is on the Clothing and Misc page, and pressing it on the hotbar shows what it holds. It weighs nothing, never leaves the pack and cannot be dropped, sold, traded or stolen by revenants. The wallet and the Deadlands Embers and Welkynd Shards it holds always stay with the player, including when they die in the Wrothgarian zone.
Wind ||| Wind has wisps, a quiet wind loop, flora that sways, wind seen and heard off one mapping, and the sky's direction. With Enhanced sounds on, outdoor wind is a soft steady rush rising and falling, not a repeated moan. The Sway setting moves both grass and trees, and turning it off holds both still. Wind changes from day to day: trees and plants sway and shiver, some days turn windy or stormy, and gusts come and go with their own sound. Leaves blow off the trees, in winter snow blows off the boughs, and the wind waits while the game is paused.
Wrothgarian Zone ||| The heart of the Wrothgarian Mountains is an open PvP zone, marked on the world map with a red border and drifting fog, with Wrothgaria itself outside it. Other players in the zone, its dungeons included, can attack except the party, guild and duel opponent; attacking a player inside a city calls the guards, and the zone's guards are ten times as strong. Foes are four times as strong and travel in packs four times the size, and the zone has four tiers from the Foothills to the Heart with loot rising +25%, +50%, +75% and +100%. On death the player keeps potions, torches, campfire kit, food, ammunition and anything that never changes hands, bags and the cart's load drop for 10 minutes, a letter of credit drops at full value, the killer may take one piece of worn gear, and the player waits two minutes to respawn in the nearest town outside the zone. Fast travel in or out is not allowed, and inside it a fast journey costs 2,500 to 15,000 gold once every 10 minutes with travel speed capped at x20 in the wilds and x40 on roads. Each tier has two Greater Giants and the zone has its own daily-changing dungeon halls, banks, house purchases, world events and revenants are restricted (one revenant at a time), and clicking the zone on the world map or pressing V opens its own map.
