Beautiful Cities ||| Beautiful Cities is a mod pair, both on by default, each with a switch under Mods that applies on the next start. City walls no longer have 3-metre gaps beside their corner towers, and the crate of belongings that comes with a moved house now shows up in the new house when the city uses the Beautiful Cities layout.
Blended Climates ||| Where two climates meet, the land no longer changes along a straight line: the border wanders and breaks into patches about two hundred metres deep, so grass thins into sand, rock creeps into the woods and snow comes in drifts. Trees follow the same border, each belonging to the climate of the ground it stands on, and the grass follows it too. The Blended climates setting is on by default and takes effect when the world next loads; off gives a straight edge, as Daggerfall does, and online it is always on so everyone sees the same trees.
Court Writs ||| Court writs are tasks posted by a region's Court, shown on the Notice Board's Work tab and paying Marks and Renown. Writs ask for herbs, metal, stone, logs and Pine Logs in some regions. A region's Court posts them once a player registered for a week has gathered there, and Take fills a writ whole from the player's Stores, up to 3 a day. A writ's Renown goes to the character who delivers it, and the day's first writ always asks for the highest tier on offer. The Work tab shows "Court writs today" at the top instead of under the last card.
Cover ||| With Enhanced AI, trees, crates, barrels, statues and solid decor are cover that blocks sight and stops missiles both ways, while area spells still burst where they land. Grass, flowers, small clutter and see-through props are not cover, and felled trees stop being cover. In winter the trees of a wood are cover the way they are the rest of the year: enemies hide behind the trunk and under the crown.
Customs ||| Customs controls bringing an older-version online character into the game. Developers can let one such character in, once, and Bring online keeps every house, ship and piece of furniture. Furniture that came with a house gives no gold back when taken out, and anything customs kept back returns when that character is played, with nothing taken to make room. Customs also counts gold and letters of credit in a sworn revenant companion's pack, the same as a crew companion's pack, where before a revenant could carry any amount across uncapped.
Enhanced Lighting ||| Enhanced lighting is a rendering overhaul with a linear pipeline, shadows in three cascades, contact shadows, depth and air, a depth-aware ambient occlusion blur, rigs that cast shadows and water that receives them. It was overhauled in 0.1.4360 (shadows, lights and post chain), and it can be turned off online. The shadow cache is on by default, so lamp-lit rooms such as taverns, shops and guild halls keep their frame rate. The shadow flicker on Windows, a strobing on some NVIDIA cards and every shadow in a room blinking at once on others, came from how cached shadows were copied on Direct3D and is fixed; the "Shadow cache (faster, may flicker)" switch is gone.
Gear Wear ||| Gear wear is how weapons and armour degrade. Wear per hit is at Daggerfall's own rate, only damage that gets through armour wears the weapon and the piece hit, good armour wears slower, and claws and bites no longer wear armour. Every weapon type now has the same base condition, the Warhammer's 1,600, with the material ladder unchanged, so daggers, tantos and short bows no longer break far sooner than a warhammer of the same metal; an iron short bow takes about 800 hits of 20 damage. Enchanted weapons gain the most: a Cast-When-Strikes enchantment costs 10 condition a strike, so an iron Worm's Tooth lasts 160 strikes, and a Cast-When-Held enchantment pays its casting cost the first time it is equipped without breaking the weapon. Weapons update on load keeping the same percentage of condition, repairs are priced by the share of condition lost so light weapons and bows cost less to keep up, and broken enchanted gear stays in the pack for a smith to repair.
Grass ||| Grass is the ground cover. Its default style is Meadow: hand-drawn tall and short green tufts, flowering tufts, dry tufts and bushes on crossed cards like the low-poly trees, each growing to its own size, with flowers in lush patches and dry grass in poor ones. It takes its colour and exactly the same light as the ground under it in every climate and season, including a texture mod's ground, stands on slopes, sways in the wind with stiffer bushes, follows the blended climate border, and far grass fades into the ground's colour. Grass is off by default; Settings > Grass turns it on at Full, Half or Quarter and picks the style, Meadow, Pixel (the old tufts) or Smooth.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, with My guild letting the character's guild members walk in. Any of a room's own furniture can be taken out of the house, the free house from a knightly order is never a building another player owns, and graveyard crypts are no longer sold as homes online because they have no inside. Online homes and guild halls on the arena's block move to a new house on next login, and the move no longer touches a home its owner already lives in or empties its storage; items lost earlier were not restored.
Market ||| The Market is a tab on every Notice Board, beside Notices and Work, where goods are traded in Marks (and later also in gold). Its views are grouped as Buy (Materials, Crafted, Auctions, Goods), Sell (Orders, My listings) and History, with the player's silver at the top, column names on listings and the Vendors list, and the Weavers' and Apothecaries' counters under one "Suppliers" line. My listings is split into the List form, the player's listings, auctions, bids and buy orders. A player lists 1 to 5,000 units at 1 to 1,000,000 Marks each, up to 30 listings of at most 10,000,000 Marks, each standing 72 hours on every board of its region, for a fee of 1% of its worth, and unsold goods come back. A sale pays at once less a 5% sales tax. A listing in the player's own region is theirs at once, and one in another region comes by courier after 15 minutes plus a minute per 10 map pixels for a fee. A signature material is cheap at home and dear abroad, History shows 7-day median prices, and Your trades lists the last 20 sales and purchases. A listing can be priced in gold or Drakes: gold has no listing fee, takes the buyer's purse, then letters of credit, then bank, and goods bought with one currency can only be resold for the same one. Worn, locked, bound, summoned and quest items, gold, boat deeds, parts, arrows and unfinished pieces cannot be listed, and dishes and jewellery have their own categories. A reported listing can be removed by a moderator. The Market starts loading as soon as the board opens.
Notice Board ||| The Notice Board is the corkboard that a town's rumour boards open, in Enhanced Plus style. Each town has one, the board nearest its middle in towns with three or more bulletin boards; the other boards are Daggerfall's rumour boards again, and Bounty Boards are unchanged. Notes stand straight on a dark panel with larger text, tabs show counts (new notes on Notices, open writs on Work), and on a phone the tab row scrolls sideways. Notices shows town and server news first, with a pointer to the Bounty Board and server notices under a red seal, then players' notes, and every board in a town shows the same notes. "N new" floats over a board with unread notes as the player approaches. A registered player can pin a note of up to 800 characters for 1, 3 or 7 days, up to 3 across every board, which can carry a button to ask to join the party or the guild, or a Challenge to a duel. Only a Guildmaster or an Officer who can invite can pin a guild recruitment note. A note can be taken down at any time by its author, any registered player can report one, and after three different reports it is hidden until a moderator looks, with moderators able to remove any note and a muted player unable to pin. Messages across the tabs are short and plain, and the board's window downloads in the background after the game starts so it is usually ready when first opened. Many fixes keep half-written forms and notes from being lost.
Off-Road Placement ||| Trees, bushes and rocks no longer stand on roads and tracks and their branches no longer hang over them, including the 3D trees from Low Poly Trees. Herb patches, ore veins on open stone and Motherlodes keep off the roads too, and forests no longer grow across dirt tracks. The Clear roadsides setting is on by default; off lets them grow up to the edge of the road, as Daggerfall does.
Profile ||| The profile has EOTB skins, a pause-menu profile (PROFILE2) and a live look. The inspect card is tidied: the name always keeps its line, badges sit on their own row under it, and the duel, breach, town and serpent records are compact plaques. Worn gear is grouped into In hand, Armour, Clothing and Jewellery, with a pair such as two rings sharing one row.
Project Legacy ||| Project Legacy, by Lattymoy, Ghð§† and Positronico, is part of the game and on by default (off on its tile in Features). Every new character founds a house with a surname and a seat in the first town they stand in, often with a brother or sister, unless the player chooses No lineage online. The player chooses how death works: Enduring costs years of life taken by Arkay and lets the line carry on to an heir, Bloodline makes death final with a Succession window to pick who takes the mantle, and No lineage (online) means no house and death as it always was. Heirs are born with stats, skills, career and race drawn from their parents' and a share of the family estate of up to 9,900 gold, and a child inherits part of a parent's reputation. Towns talk about the house, and the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online the family lives in the realm and the house's name shows under the player's name, with a skull marking a Bloodline; characters made before the update have no house online, and a family that has not changed since the last checkpoint is no longer uploaded again. Permadeath cannot be undone by quick-loading, closing the game or a second tab, and a fallen character's Stores, professions and market place fall with them. An heir's bequest gets the same load fixes as other items when it is paid, and a fallen relative's remains get them when opened. See also Heirlooms, Courtship and Marriage, Family in the World and Family Tab.
Saves ||| Loading after an online rest no longer resets hunger and thirst for free, and a save with a damaged clock loads safely instead of freezing the game. On load, older items get their fixes wherever the save keeps them, including things set out in the house, quest items, a revenant's takings and a sworn revenant's pack, enemies' and guards' gear, and Project Legacy's bequests and remains. The fixes move weapons to the new condition pool, give items made with no condition one, restore the Brigandine, Fur or Mail word on Roleplay & Realism armour, name old Sigil Stones Deadlands Embers, and turn a rolled jewel on an unwearable Wand into an Amulet. A rolled Wand set out in the house becomes an Amulet but still looks like a wand where it stands until picked up.
Seat Tab ||| A Notice Board's Seat tab shows the week's standings, pledged guilds, the player's guild's pledge and their week, the holder, this week's siege or Tourney, claim costs and the Chronicle. It shows the Tithe and this week's Edict, and members also see next week's Edict, upkeep and debt. A crown's tab shows its Saturday siege slot instead of the holder's window. The guild war is split into panels (the Charter, This week with Muster, Reckoning and Turning, the battle, Standings, Your guild, the holder's orders, the Works and the Chronicle), each challenging guild has a bar showing how close it is to claiming the Charter or winning the Right of Siege, and a "How the seat war works" guide covers pledging, earning influence, claiming, sieges and holding. The tab loads its standings and works at the same time.
Wallet ||| Every character carries a Wallet, which new characters start with and existing ones received on next load. It holds letters of credit, Deadlands Embers and Welkynd Shards and shows gold and, online, silver. Its sheet is a ledger: Coin (gold, silver) and In the wallet (letters of credit, Deadlands Embers, Welkynd Shards), with each amount lined up on the right. In the Enhanced inventory the three move to the Wallet on Valuables, in the classic inventory nothing moves and the Wallet is on the Clothing and Misc page, and pressing it on the hotbar shows what it holds. It weighs nothing, never leaves the pack and cannot be dropped, sold, traded or stolen by revenants.
Word Filter ||| Slurs and crude words are starred out in chat on every channel, Notice Board notes, guild notes, letters and shared journal pages, while the rest of the message and ordinary words that only contain a filtered one are left alone. Notes pinned before the filter are starred too when a board is read. A guild rank name or a ship's name with such a word is refused and the message names the word, a custom class name with one is not shown to other players, a maker's mark with one is left off the item, and a name given with the Item Maker is starred wherever the item is shown.
