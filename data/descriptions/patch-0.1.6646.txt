Accounts ||| Accounts hold a player's login and their cloud saves, served by an account Worker that issues tokens and checks passwords. The service was built in 0.1.3607 and 0.1.3690, then repaired after an outage caused by an overly costly password-hashing setting on Cloudflare, and later given a clock fix. The account server has since been protected from overload: after it fell over again in 0.1.6625 games now wait longer after each failed ask instead of retrying at once, and in 0.1.6640 signing in, joining a room and loading a town's house layouts were made far cheaper for it.
Enhanced Lighting ||| Enhanced lighting is a rendering overhaul with a linear pipeline, shadows in three cascades, contact shadows, depth and air, a depth-aware ambient occlusion blur, rigs that cast shadows and water that receives them. It was overhauled in 0.1.4360 (shadows, lights and post chain), and it can be turned off online. Shadows no longer blink all at once in some rooms, because the shadow cache behind them is now off by default and can be turned back on under Enhanced Lighting with "Shadow cache (faster, may flicker)".
Foe Health Bar ||| A foe's health bar no longer shows a dark or empty box under it. The poise bar appears only when there is something to show, a resting foe shows only its wind-up track, and the bar has one shadow instead of two.
Living World ||| The living world makes every town's people residents with a name, face, home and trade, kept for good. Each has a day (rise, work, errands, friends, evening, home) with the watch keeping shifts, townsfolk stop and talk among themselves, and people whose day has them there are inside taverns, shops, temples and houses. Townspeople belong to the guild of their trade or class and spend an evening at their guild hall twice a week, errands go to shops the person has business at, and travellers staying at a tavern, sailors ashore and adventurers who live there each have a room by one of its beds, which a room the player rents never takes. Merchants, pilgrims, couriers, pedlars and adventurers travel between towns by day and camp by night, meeting monsters, sailors crew ships in port towns and parties on the road are marked on the Overworld. Adventurers dive nearby dungeons and fight at the player's side, some never come up and leave remains with a keepsake. Kindness, help, theft or crime change how people feel: a friend greets the player by name and aids them while an enemy will not speak, and striking down a resident is permanent, turning their household against the player. It is on by default under Features (World) and off, or on Classic UI, towns keep Daggerfall's townsfolk. In 0.1.6640 walled cities got their streets back: 407 of the 410 cities had nobody on their streets because townspeople were laid out outside the walls, and that is fixed.
Login ||| A crash some online players hit right after logging in was fixed, and logging in no longer sends the player back to the title screen after the account service fell over under load. In 0.1.6566 logins stopped failing for everyone after the account server was overwhelmed by every game checking every town's yard decorations each minute: yards are now fetched only for towns near the player, the server keeps a short copy, and a failed fetch waits longer each time. Signing into a room after a slow server answer no longer asks for a second sign-in ticket, so the player reconnects sooner.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera.
Project Legacy ||| Project Legacy, by Lattymoy, Ghð§† and Positronico, is part of the game and on by default (off on its tile in Features). Every new character founds a house with a surname and a seat in the first town they stand in, often with a brother or sister, unless the player chooses No lineage online. The player chooses how death works: Enduring costs years of life taken by Arkay and lets the line carry on to an heir, Bloodline makes death final with a Succession window to pick who takes the mantle, and No lineage (online) means no house and death as it always was. Heirs are born with stats, skills, career and race drawn from their parents' and a share of the family estate of up to 9,900 gold, and a child inherits part of a parent's reputation. Towns talk about the house, and the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online the family lives in the realm and the house's name shows under the player's name, with a skull marking a Bloodline; characters made before the update have no house online, and a family that has not changed since the last checkpoint is no longer uploaded again. Permadeath cannot be undone by quick-loading, closing the game or a second tab, and a fallen character's Stores, professions and market place fall with them. See also Heirlooms, Courtship and Marriage, Family in the World and Family Tab.
Quests ||| Quests received many fixes. A quest monster killed by a party member counts for the player's quest, a quest's monsters are the player's own and players outside the party cannot see or hit them, and a quest's hours, date and season follow the sky with a daily window recurring every 30 real minutes. Quest-givers are back, every party member's copy of a shared quest picks the same random monsters, and quest items in the world show their name when looked at. Quests that would not start in towns redesigned by Beautiful Villages and Beautiful Cities now start by using the nearest town that has a missing building, quest characters whose building is gone live in a house of their own town, and shared party quests no longer move to another building on every sync. Individual quests fixed include the Bodyguard quest, a knightly order's artifact hunt, the Totem of Tiber Septim, The Qualifying Examination, The Possessed Child, Pothago's Dark Brotherhood contract and, online, Protect an Honored Mage, whose three-hour guard now lasts its full three hours of play with the attack always coming inside it.
Steady Shadows ||| Steady Shadows is a switch in the Enhanced lighting tile, on by default, that stops shadows popping on and off by redrawing every shadow every frame, and since 0.1.6646 they also hold steady through a brief hitch instead of flashing. The nearest lamps keeping full shadows went from 8 to 12, lamps at almost the same distance no longer trade shadows, the player's own shadow no longer jumps from lamp to lamp, and eyes adjust to light changes more slowly. It costs graphics work and about 48 MB of memory, so it can be turned off if frame rate drops, and the player's silhouette falls only from the two nearest lamps. Lantern shadows cost much less to prepare, since each lantern looks once at what can reach it and a still scene does not look at all.
Town Watch ||| The town watch keeps the town day and night in three eight-hour shifts plus a day off. Patrols walk in pairs around their own district, in larger towns a watchman stands at each gate, off duty he wears his own clothes as a townsperson, and larger towns keep a larger watch. In the 14 cities whose palace stands in its own walled grounds the watch now lives where its door opens onto the streets, so it walks its patrols instead of staying at the palace. A watchman on duty greets the player by their standing with the region's law, a first minor offence in a region draws a Hold! warning and not an arrest (the next brings one), and the watch shouts "Halt!" from one watchman at most once every 15 seconds. A monster that gets into town can attack townspeople, one blow killing one, and the watch defends them as it does the player. Paying a fine no longer counts as killing the watchman who walked away. All of this is in the living world, and Classic play keeps Daggerfall's own watch and townspeople.
Townspeople ||| Townspeople in the living world run from a monster that comes close, along the street rather than through walls, and keep their distance. Those who stop to talk turn to face each other, a group waits until all have walked over and then takes turns, and the player hears them only then. People keep the same company about two minutes before moving on, with much less repetition, and indoors people at a table start talking as soon as they sit together. A stranger who said nothing as the player passed now speaks when the player stops in front of them, and a townsperson who stops for the player stays exactly where they stopped. They no longer stand inside walls or out over the harbour water or walk on the spot, and since 0.1.6640 they keep room from one another: people waiting at one spot keep a little distance, two leaving a shop together walk out in single file, and a walker steps aside, keeping right. Each square has several gathering points and every neighbourhood has a corner where its people meet, stall-keepers set up at the square or their own market, and people head out for the evening and the morning market at their own time. Guild members, priests, adventurers, sellswords and couriers walk the streets in their class, and someone keeping their place on their own appears as one of Daggerfall's own pictures of their kind, becoming themselves again the moment they walk on or join a conversation. A cat that meowed many times a second while the player looked at certain townspeople was fixed.
Travel Options ||| Travel options set how journeys run. Fare scaling counts the player's Mercantile, so a good haggler pays less, a journey stops for a hostile ship including a crossing by sea, and changing the settings takes effect straight away. A Player Controlled Inns Travel dial, on by default, makes any trip stopping at inns a journey, and online every trip over land on the travel map is travelled, not instant, with its options always on. Two speed dials returned in 0.1.6646 after being removed in 0.1.6444: one for the journey's speed and one for the speed while enemies are near, each going x5, x10, x20, x30, x40 and x60, with x100 on a road only. Leaving a road drops a dial at x100 to x60 at once and it does not climb back by itself, journeys start at x60, and the dials are in every interface (the Overworld's panel, the Enhanced journey bar, and a small speed box with the classic interface and GrimoireUI).
Waypoints ||| Waypoints are map markers placed by right-clicking the Overworld or the world map (V). Each has a name and a flag colour and is set for the player alone, their party or their guild. Party and guild waypoints are shared with the other members, and only the player who placed one can change it for everyone, while anyone can rename or remove one on their own screen. A waypoint can be followed from the Overworld's filters, where it stays marked at the edge of the screen with its distance, and its own menu can send the player travelling there. Leaving a party or a guild removes its waypoints.
